Temporal entities and relations

The basic structure of the ontology is based on a calculus of binary relations on intervals developed by Allen for representing qualitative temporal information, and to address the problem of reasoning about such information ([al-83]], [[ak-83]], [[al-84]], [[af-97]]). The relationships between temporal entities can be defined in terms of before and identity on the beginning and end points.

Temporal entities

The ontology starts with a class :TemporalEntity with properties :hasBeginning and :hasEnd that link to the temporal instants that define its limits, and :hasTemporalDuration to describe its extent (). There are two subclasses: :Interval and :Instant, and they are the only two subclasses of :TemporalEntity.

Intervals are things with temporal extent. Instants are point-like in that they have no interior points, but it is generally safe to think of an instant as an interval with zero length, where the beginning and end are the same.

The class :Interval has one subclass :ProperInterval, which corresponds with the common understanding of intervals, in that the beginning and end are distinct, and whose membership is therefore disjoint from :Instant.

Five basic relations between temporal entities and instants are shown, which are implemented in OWL-Time as:

  • :hasBeginning , :hasEnd
  • :before → inverse :after
  • :inside

The arrangement linking intervals and instants into a class hierarchy, and the corresponding relations, were introduced by Hobbs & Pan ([[hp-04]]) to supplement the Allen's work which dealt solely with proper intervals.

Allen's interval relations

Relationships between intervals are the primary focus of Allen's analysis ([[al-83]], [[ak-83]], [[al-84]], [[af-97]]). Thirteen elementary relations are the key contribution (). These relations support unambiguous expression of all possible relationships between temporal entities, which allows the computation of any relative position or sequence.

The basic interval relations are implemented in OWL-Time as:

  • :intervalBefore → inverse of :intervalAfter
  • :intervalMeets → inverse of :intervalMetBy
  • :intervalOverlaps → inverse of :intervalOverlappedBy
  • :intervalStarts → inverse of :intervalStartedBy
  • :intervalDuring → inverse of :intervalContains
  • :intervalFinishes → inverse of :intervalFinishedBy
  • :intervalEquals

Note that the standard interval calculus assumes all intervals are proper, so their beginning and end are different.

Additional relations between temporal entities

In principle, any relationship between temporal entities can be built up through logical combination of members of the primitive relations. In practice, there are limitations in the expressivity OWL2, particularly around unions and complements of properties [[owl2-direct-semantics]][[owl2-rdf-based-semantics]]. In OWL2 the union of properties can only be approximated through a set of sub-property axioms, which link the members up to the union, but does not exclude the possibility of other sub-properties being added to the union.

Due to these considerations it is convenient to introduce some additional topological relations between temporal entities explicitly. This supports some applications more efficiently, and is useful for interoperability purposes.

Four additional relations between proper intervals are implemented in OWL-Time:

  • :intervalIn → union of :intervalDuring, :intervalStarts, :intervalFinishes
  • :intervalHasInside → union of :intervalContains, :intervalStartedBy, :intervalFinishedBy
  • :intervalDisjoint → union of :intervalBefore, :intervalAfter, :intervalMeets, :intervalMetBy
  • :intervalNotDisjoint → union of :intervalEquals, :intervalHasInside, :intervalIn, :intervalOverlaps, :intervalOverlappedBy

:intervalHasInside (and its inverse :intervalIn) is implied by the following mention in [[ak-83]]: "a relationship that will be useful later is the notion that one interval A wholly contains another interval B. We shall assert A [wholly] contains B as an abbreviation for A (contains|startedBy|finishedBy) B.".

:intervalDisjoint is implied by the following mention in [[ak-83]]: "the fact that intervals A and B are disjoint can be expressed by asserting that A is before, meets, is met by, or is after B". :intervalNotDisjoint is the complement of :intervalDisjoint.

The fact that `meets` is disjoint implies that Allen does not consider the instants at the end and beginning of an interval to be part of the interval - i.e., Allen intervals are 'open'.

Finally, four additional relations between general temporal entities support the description of all possible relationships between temporal entities, which allows the computation of any relative position or sequence.

  • :hasInside → union of :inside, :intervalHasInside
  • :equals → super-property of :intervalEquals,
    but not restricted to relations between proper intervals
  • :notDisjoint → union of :equals, :hasBeginning, :hasEnd, :hasInside, :intervalNotDisjoint,
    complement of :disjoint
  • :disjoint → union of :before, :after, :intervalDisjoint

Relating temporal information with any entity

The properties :hasTemporalDuration, :hasBeginning and :hasEnd, together with a fourth generic property :hasTime, support the association of temporal information with any entity, such as an activity or event. These may be used directly in applications if suitable, or specialized if needed.
